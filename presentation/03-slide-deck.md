## Slide 12 — Universal principle: IKEA effect (+ Gall’s Law)

**On slide**
- **IKEA effect:** When you keep and edit *your* version, ownership rises → Intention to use rises
- Evidence in UI: My version badge · whyKept · notes · “Blog called for…” vs your amounts
- **Gall’s Law (brief):** We started with three screens (Kept). We grew keep → find → plan → cook once the core worked — not a big-bang full kitchen OS

**Claim:** Ownership of the kept recipe is a designed outcome, not decoration.  
**Speaker:** William (IKEA) · Ethan or William (Gall, 15s)  
**Notes:** 50s. Show My version screenshot.

## Slide 21 — Implications for design

**On slide**
What we changed after testing:
- **My version** visible on recipe + cook (William)
- Cook: **next-step peek** without gutting step focus (George)
- **My recipes** labeling / personal catalogue (Caleb)

## Slide 22 — Iterations, success, limits, next steps

**On slide**
- **Success:** Cook tracking and findability beat usual methods; control story is clearer
- **Limits:** Real import (scrape/OCR) still the adoption cliff; Plan Ahead was the pain surface; kitchen/phone readiness incomplete
- **If we continued:** Real URL/photo intake · mobile cook (wake lock) · trustworthy grocery editability

**Claim:** We’re frank about what worked and what didn’t.  
**Speaker:** William

## Slide 23 — Ethics

**On slide**
Going forward we would:
1. Treat recipe content as the user’s — clear ownership, export, delete
2. Be explicit about analytics (e.g. PostHog): consent, what’s collected
3. Not overclaim automated scrape/OCR accuracy
4. Design household sharing with permission boundaries

**Claim:** Ethics shape the next design process, not a slide of buzzwords.  
**Speaker:** William  
**Notes:** 30s. Then: “Questions?” → 3 min Q&A.
